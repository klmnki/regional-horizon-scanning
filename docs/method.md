# The scanning method

## The daily unit of work

A run has a local date, a research cutoff, eight coverage records, zero or more selected signals or analytical products, and a briefing. The six focal countries are cases to follow; they are not a statistically representative sample of the region.

Search each country in its relevant languages. Use English as a complement. Record languages attempted and successfully read, query/source families, access failures and the actual time window. Local-language query terms alone do not prove that a local-language source was read.

News starts with the previous 24 hours. Widen an individual sparse lane to 72 hours and then at most seven days. Analytical products use 24 hours, then seven days, with a separate 30-day discovery check for missed work. Useful older research must be explicitly labelled **Analytical baseline**. Keep publication or material-update date, event/data period and first-seen date separate. Unknown dates stay unknown.

Use statistical offices, central banks, public institutions, employers, worker organisations, universities, youth and civil society, independent reporting and relevant business ecosystems. Avoid making the scan a list of government announcements and vendor claims.

## Select evidence, not headlines

Open original sources where available. Record what was actually read: full text, abstract, excerpt or secondary reporting. Build a candidate pool before selecting. Exclude untraceable claims and unchanged duplicates. Compare underlying events, actors and paper/version families as well as URLs. Several syndicated articles do not provide independent corroboration.

| Criterion | Points | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relevance | 0–3 | 3: direct territorial AI-economic link; 2: explicit transmission link; 1: justified context; 0: no relevant pathway |
| Novelty | 0–2 | 0: unchanged duplicate; 1: meaningful incremental change; 2: new or reframing observation |
| Strategic economic importance | 0–2 | 0: marginal; 1: sectoral or plausible significance; 2: material systemic stakes |
| Youth/distribution | 0–1 | 1 when the relevance to unequal gains, burdens or entry pathways is explicit |
| Evidence/corroboration | 0–2 | 0: untraceable, exclude; 1: credible but limited; 2: primary measurement or credible independent corroboration |

Record the components and total out of ten. There is no universal pass score: selection also considers diversity, novelty and the limitations of the evidence. Keep at most three items per lane, at most 24 across the run. This is a ceiling. A well-researched empty lane is valid.

## Follow the economic mechanism

Use one primary mechanism and no more than two secondary ones.

| Code | Mechanism | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | Tasks and entry pathways | Substitution, augmentation, junior work, mentoring, learning and verification |
| E2 | Productivity and demand | Costs, quality, prices, output, new products and resulting labour demand |
| E3 | Value capture | Domestic wages, profits, taxes, ownership, intellectual property and payments abroad |
| E4 | Firm access and competition | SME costs, finance, diffusion, concentration and new entrants |
| E5 | Work and distribution | Pay, hours, bargaining, informality, security and unequal gains or burdens |
| E6 | Cross-border transmission | Outsourcing, exports, migration, remote work, remittances and external demand |
| E7 | Enabling constraints | Skills, languages, data, compute, connectivity, energy and institutional capacity |
| E8 | Public economic choices | Procurement, fiscal costs, dependency, service productivity, oversight and exit options |

Identify the furthest stage the evidence supports:

**Capability → adoption → task/cost/quality change → prices/output → employment/income → distribution.**

Exposure is not adoption. Task automation does not establish job losses. Time saved does not establish firm-wide productivity. Productivity gains do not establish wage growth. Investment and export revenue do not establish domestic value retained. Document competing explanations, such as conflict, inflation or broader restructuring.

## What makes a signal regional?

A regional record must name the affected places and the mechanism connecting them. Examples of pathways to investigate include service-export demand, shared platforms, migration and remittances, capital flows, energy constraints, language markets or policy spillovers. These are search directions, not presumed findings.

Use one canonical record with several territory tags for one multi-country event. Tags express relevance; **evidence geography** states where the observation or sample actually comes from. A conference venue or company headquarters is not evidence of economic impact in that country.

For global research, write a transfer test containing:

1. Relevant local sectors, tasks or populations.
2. Conditions needed for the reported mechanism to operate.
3. Important differences from the study's sample or model.
4. Local evidence still needed.

Classify applicability as direct evidence, a transfer hypothesis or comparative context. Do not apply a global exposure percentage to a country without an appropriate local basis.

## Build interpretations over time

Keep these concepts distinct:

- **Weak signal:** an early, uncertain observation that may point towards change.
- **Emerging-issue indicator:** evidence of a developing question or tension.
- **Driver indicator:** an observation relevant to a sustained force; one item alone does not establish that force.
- **Wild card:** a plausible discontinuity with potentially large consequences.
- **Context:** useful background, explicitly distinguished from new change.

A synthesis record links canonical evidence IDs and specifies its geography, independent cases, counterevidence, confidence and what would weaken it. Emerging issues normally need at least two independent observations. Any exception needs an explicit rationale. Drivers need a pattern across time and causal reasoning. An uncertainty must allow at least two plausible directions.

Update synthesis only when evidence strengthens, weakens, splits or reframes it. Compare the preceding seven days each Monday; review accumulated interpretations on the first working day of the month using the operator's holiday calendar. Reconsider mechanisms and contradictions, not just headline counts. New records remain Unreviewed until a person reviews them.

## Brief and verify

Save selected evidence and all coverage records before preparing delivery. The briefing has a coverage line, up to five evidence-linked takeaways, each country, regional developments, research products, cross-scan interpretation and one grounded question. Include every selected item, source and record ID. Use an honest one-line status for empty or inaccessible lanes.

Re-read saved values, source URLs and affected table ranges. Record failures. If delivery is enabled, record its state separately from research and archive completion; verify a sent message before claiming it was sent. Ambiguous sends require checking Sent before retrying. Sending is not proof of inbox delivery.
