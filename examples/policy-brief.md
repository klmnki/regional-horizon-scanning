# Worked example: turning an observation into an argument

**Fictional demonstration. No company, country, event or empirical finding is represented.**

## Starting observation

A fictional service operator reports that an automated assistant handled routine requests. The record does not establish whether difficult cases were resolved or whether staff time was reassigned.

## Weak draft

“AI improves efficiency and creates opportunities, but it also presents risks. Policymakers should ensure that everyone benefits.”

This names generic possibilities without showing who gains time, who does the remaining work, or what determines the outcome.

## Questions that improve the argument

- What is observed? Routine automated contacts, not verified resolutions or net time saved.
- What must happen for a service benefit to follow? The answer must resolve the need, or an effective route to human assistance must remain.
- What might change in the distribution of work? Staff could handle difficult cases, or users could inherit more searching and correction.
- What would distinguish those outcomes? Completion rates, unresolved cases, user effort, access to an authorised person and outcomes for different user groups.

## Example section after revision

### Access to help after the automated answer

**When routine contact is automated, the ability to reach someone who can resolve an exception may become more important.** In this fictional example, a service operator reports handling routine requests through an assistant. That claim establishes contact volume, not successful resolution.

If the operator redirects staff time to difficult cases, automation could improve support. If it reduces human assistance, users may have to do more searching and correction themselves. People with straightforward requests could benefit while those whose circumstances need an exception face a harder route. The next evidence to seek is whether people obtain a resolution, how much effort they spend, and whether someone with authority remains accessible.

## What the revision changes

It identifies a specific possible shift in responsibility, explains two mechanisms, keeps the observation separate from the hypothesis and names evidence that could distinguish outcomes. It makes no regional prevalence or policy-effectiveness claim. In a real brief, replace this fictional source with verified evidence; do not publish it as a finding.
